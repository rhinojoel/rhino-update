Rhino's Take

Public Liability Insurance is a core policy for tradespeople and helps cover your business should accidents happen on site.

It protects your business financially should a member of the public suffer injury, or third-party property is damaged as a result of your work. You're covered up to your chosen level of cover.

Whilst not a legal requirement, it's often required for contract work and accreditations, and most customers are unlikely to work with you without it.

What's covered
  • Accidental injury to a customer or member of the public
  • Accidental damage to a customer's property
  • Legal costs and expenses linked to a compensation claim
  • Accidental death of a third party caused by your work
What's not covered
  • Injury to your staff (that's Employers' Liability Insurance)
  • Theft of your tools (that's Tools Cover)
  • Financial loss from poor advice or designs (that's Professional Indemnity Insurance)
  • Injury to you while working (that's Personal Accident Insurance)
Excess and claims
  • There's an excess of £500 on a claim.
  • It rises to £1,000 if the claim is a direct result of the use of heat.
  • To make a claim, call or email us and we'll start the process with you.

What is Public Liability Insurance?

Cover that pays out if your work injures someone or damages their property.

When will Public Liability Insurance cover me?

Public Liability Insurance will protect you financially if a client, member of the public, supplier, visitor or customer suffers an injury, illness, or damage to their property because of your business activities.

It will also cover you in the worst-case scenario: accidental death of a member of the public that is caused by your business activities. Public Liability Insurance will cover legal expenses and/or compensation owed by your business in the event of a claim.

Rhino offers levels of cover of £1M, £2M, and £5M. Workplace accidents aren't rare, and there is always the possibility that you could cause damage or harm to third parties whilst you are working.

Our Public Liability Insurance also adds value to your business, as you can prove to potential customers or contractors that you are a reliable, safe worker who can cover the costs if there's any injuries or property damage whilst on the job.

What does it cover?

Injury, damage and the legal bill that follows.

What happens if someone makes a claim against me?

Rhino's Public Liability Insurance will insure your business against the cost of a third party bringing legal action against you for injury, death or property damage caused by your business activities.

It doesn't matter who the claimant is, whether they are a customer of yours or a contractor you are working for, you're still covered with Rhino. The policy will cover the legal costs and compensation associated with the claim up to the limit of indemnity chosen by you.

Rhino's Public Liability Insurance policies cover Public/Product Liability and Pollution Liability Insurance as standard, and cover can be purchased for work up to 15 metres in height and to depth limits down to 2 metres.

Getting cover for your business with Rhino is easy. You can use our online quote engine, or call our friendly, expert team for a chat on 0116 243 7904.

Did you know?

Public Liability Insurance isn't a legal requirement for a UK business, but most customers won't work with you without it. If you take on employees, Employers' Liability Insurance becomes the legal requirement instead.

What affects the cost of my cover?

Public Liability Insurance in the UK starts from £5.21 a month with Rhino. Prices are calculated depending on the level of risk your business poses and depends on several factors.

Commonly, your trade dictates the premium of your liability insurance. Those working in lower-risk trades, such as cleaning or carpentry will usually pay less for their Public Liability Insurance than someone working on gas systems, doing plumbing work or digging up groundworks.

Another factor that affects the premium is the number of people you are covering. Chances are the more people you have working on a project, the more likely the event of a claim. Other factors can include your business's turnover, the level of cover you wish to take out and in some cases, the locations where you are working.

Do you need Employers' Liability Insurance?

Employers' Liability Insurance protects the policyholder in the event of a claim being brought against them by one of their own employees, and is a legal requirement for any business that hires paid employees, although there are some exceptions to this which you can read about in our Ultimate Guide to Employers' Liability Insurance.

Employers' Liability Insurance will be added to your quote if you select cover for more than one employee. If you believe that you are exempt from this, please use the chat function to speak to a member of the team or give us a call on 0116 243 7904.

Is Public Liability Insurance a legal requirement?

Public Liability Insurance isn't a legal requirement, but it protects your business from the cost of settling a claim up to your chosen level of cover. Many contractors and customers will ask to see proof of cover before offering you work, so having it in place can also help your reputation and win you more jobs.

Do sole traders need Public Liability Insurance?

Public Liability Insurance isn't a legal requirement for sole traders. However, if a client or member of the public is injured, or their property damaged, because of your work, you could be liable for the cost. Without Public Liability Insurance in place, you would need to cover those costs yourself. Many clients and contractors expect to see cover in place before offering work.

What is the difference between Public Liability Insurance and Employers' Liability Insurance?

Public Liability Insurance covers claims from clients, customers or members of the public for injury or property damage. Employers' Liability Insurance covers claims from your own employees, temporary or permanent, should they fall ill or become injured as a result of working for your business. Employers' Liability Insurance is also a legal requirement if you employ anyone.

Do I need Public Liability Insurance if I am self-employed?

It's not a legal requirement, but if your work brings you into contact with customers or the public, it's worth considering. Should you cause injury to a member of the public or damage to a third-party property as a result of your work, you will need to cover compensation costs and legal fees out of your own pocket. Public Liability Insurance helps cover those costs up to your chosen level of cover.

What is the excess for Public Liability Insurance?

Rhino's Public Liability Insurance carries a standard excess of £500, meaning you pay the first £500 of any claim and your insurance covers the rest (up to your chosen level of cover). For example, on a £20,750 claim, you'd pay £500 and your insurer would cover £20,250. This rises to £1,000 for claims involving heat, such as gas engineering, or damage to underground services. Excess Protection is also available for £25.00, which reimburses you for the excess paid should you make a claim.

What type of work does Public Liability Insurance cover you for?

Rhino's Public Liability Insurance covers both commercial and domestic work, unless stated otherwise, so you can operate across both environments with confidence that you're covered.

What locations does Public Liability Insurance cover?

Rhino Trade Insurance covers work carried out in the United Kingdom only, including Northern Ireland and the Channel Islands. Work done outside the UK isn't covered. Cover also excludes docks, harbours, railways, watercraft, offshore gas or oil installations, chemical or petrochemical works, oil or gas refineries or storage facilities, aircraft, airports, airfields, collieries, mines, quarries, power stations, and any installation where nuclear processing takes place.
